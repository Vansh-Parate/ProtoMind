# Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) — Key Compliance Requirements

## Overview
The Bank Secrecy Act (BSA), enacted in 1970 and amended by subsequent legislation including the USA PATRIOT Act (2001) and the Anti-Money Laundering Act (2020), requires U.S. financial institutions to assist government agencies in detecting and preventing money laundering.

## Core Requirements

### 1. Customer Identification Program (CIP)
- Verify the identity of any person seeking to open an account
- Maintain records of the information used to verify identity
- Determine whether the person appears on any government lists of known or suspected terrorists or terrorist organizations
- **Reference:** 31 CFR § 1020.220

### 2. Customer Due Diligence (CDD) / Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)
- Identify and verify the identity of beneficial owners of legal entity customers (25%+ ownership)
- Understand the nature and purpose of customer relationships
- Conduct ongoing monitoring to maintain and update customer information
- Apply enhanced due diligence for higher-risk customers (PEPs, high-risk jurisdictions, complex structures)
- **Reference:** 31 CFR § 1020.230

### 3. Currency Transaction Reports (CTR)
- File CTR for each deposit, withdrawal, exchange, or transfer involving more than $10,000 in currency
- File within 15 calendar days of the transaction
- Multiple transactions by or on behalf of the same person totaling more than $10,000 in a single business day must be aggregated
- **Reference:** 31 CFR § 1010.311

### 4. Suspicious Activity Reports (SAR)
- File SAR for transactions of $5,000 or more when the institution knows, suspects, or has reason to suspect that the transaction:
  - Involves funds from illegal activity
  - Is designed to evade BSA reporting requirements (structuring)
  - Has no business or apparent lawful purpose
  - Involves use of the institution to facilitate criminal activity
- File within 30 calendar days of initial detection (60 days if no suspect identified)
- SARs are confidential — do not notify the subject of the report
- **Reference:** 31 CFR § 1020.320

### 5. Record Retention
- Maintain records of transactions for 5 years
- Retain CIP/CDD documentation for 5 years after account closure
- **Reference:** 31 CFR § 1010.430

## SAR Filing Best Practices
1. **Timeliness:** File within regulatory deadlines; do not delay to gather more information
2. **Completeness:** Include all known information about the subject and transactions
3. **Clarity:** Write in clear, factual language understandable to law enforcement
4. **Specificity:** Provide specific dates, amounts, account numbers, and transaction details
5. **Objectivity:** Describe facts and observations, not conclusions about criminal intent
6. **Continuing Activity:** File follow-up SARs every 90 days for ongoing suspicious activity

## Penalties for Non-Compliance
- Civil penalties: Up to $1 million per violation per day
- Criminal penalties: Up to $500,000 fine and 10 years imprisonment for willful violations
- Regulatory actions: Cease-and-desist orders, enforcement actions, loss of banking charter
