# FATF Recommendations — Summary for AML Compliance

## Overview
The Financial Action Task Force (FATF) is an inter-governmental body that sets international standards for combating money laundering, terrorist financing, and proliferation financing. The FATF 40 Recommendations provide the framework adopted by over 200 jurisdictions worldwide.

## Key Recommendations for SAR Analysis

### Recommendation 1 — Risk-Based Approach
- Countries and financial institutions should identify, assess, and understand their ML/TF risks
- Apply a risk-based approach to allocate resources and implement preventive measures
- Higher-risk situations require enhanced measures; lower-risk may allow simplified measures

### Recommendation 10 — Customer Due Diligence (CDD)
- Identify the customer and verify identity using reliable, independent documents
- Identify the beneficial owner and take reasonable measures to verify identity
- Understand the purpose and intended nature of the business relationship
- Conduct ongoing due diligence on the business relationship

### Recommendation 20 — Reporting of Suspicious Transactions
- Financial institutions should be required to report to the FIU when they suspect or have reasonable grounds to suspect that funds are the proceeds of criminal activity
- Reports should be made promptly and include all available information
- Attempted transactions should also be reported

### Recommendation 21 — Tipping Off and Confidentiality
- Financial institutions, their directors, officers, and employees should be protected from criminal and civil liability for filing suspicious transaction reports in good faith
- Prohibited from disclosing ("tipping off") that a STR/SAR has been filed

### Recommendation 26 — Financial Intelligence Units (FIUs)
- Countries should establish FIUs that serve as national centres for receiving and analyzing:
  - Suspicious transaction reports
  - Other information relevant to money laundering and terrorist financing

## Risk Categories (FATF Guidance)

### Higher Risk Factors
- **Customer:** PEPs, customers from high-risk jurisdictions, complex ownership structures
- **Product/Service:** Private banking, anonymous transactions, new technologies
- **Geography:** Countries with inadequate AML/CFT systems, conflict zones, tax havens
- **Transaction:** Unusually large or complex, no apparent economic purpose, cash-intensive

### Lower Risk Factors
- **Customer:** Government entities, regulated financial institutions, publicly listed companies
- **Product/Service:** Low-value insurance, regulated pension funds
- **Geography:** Countries with effective AML/CFT systems

## High-Risk Jurisdictions (FATF Grey/Black Lists)
- Institutions must apply enhanced due diligence for transactions involving countries identified by FATF as having strategic AML/CFT deficiencies
- The FATF list is updated three times per year (February, June, October)
- Current lists should be consulted at: https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/countries/black-and-grey-lists.html
