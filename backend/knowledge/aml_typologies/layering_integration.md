# Layering and Integration

**Definition:** Layering involves creating complex layers of financial transactions to obscure the audit trail and distance illicit funds from their source. Integration is the final stage where laundered funds re-enter the legitimate economy.

## Layering Indicators
- Rapid movement of funds between multiple accounts, often across jurisdictions
- Use of shell companies, trusts, or nominee accounts
- Wire transfers to and from high-risk jurisdictions
- Round-trip transactions (funds sent out and returned through different channels)
- Conversion between asset classes (cash → crypto → wire → real estate)
- Use of correspondent banking relationships to obscure origin

## Integration Indicators
- Purchase of high-value assets (real estate, luxury goods, art)
- Investment in legitimate businesses with high cash turnover
- Loan-back schemes where laundered funds are "loaned" back to the launderer
- Over/under invoicing of goods and services in trade-based laundering

## Common Layering Techniques
1. **Wire Transfer Chains:** Funds moved through 3+ intermediary accounts before reaching destination
2. **Shell Company Networks:** Multiple entities in different jurisdictions used to pass funds
3. **Trade-Based Laundering:** Over/under-invoicing, phantom shipments, multiple invoicing
4. **Crypto Mixing:** Use of cryptocurrency tumblers or mixers to break the transaction trail

## Risk Assessment
- **High Risk:** Multiple rapid cross-border transfers involving high-risk jurisdictions with no apparent business purpose
- **Medium Risk:** Complex corporate structures with some legitimate business activity
- **Low Risk:** Standard multi-step business transactions with clear documentation
