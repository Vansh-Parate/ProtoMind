# Structuring / Smurfing

**Definition:** Structuring, also known as "smurfing," involves breaking down large financial transactions into smaller amounts to evade reporting thresholds (e.g., the $10,000 Currency Transaction Report threshold in the United States).

## Key Indicators
- Multiple cash deposits or withdrawals just below the reporting threshold (e.g., $9,500–$9,999)
- Transactions conducted at multiple branches or ATMs within a short period
- Use of multiple accounts to aggregate funds
- Deposits made by multiple individuals into a single account
- Patterns of transactions that appear designed to avoid triggering automated alerts

## Common Patterns
1. **Classic Structuring:** A single individual makes multiple deposits of $9,000–$9,900 over several days
2. **Smurfing Network:** Multiple individuals ("smurfs") each deposit small amounts into different accounts, which are then consolidated
3. **Cross-Branch Structuring:** Deposits made at different branches of the same bank on the same day
4. **ATM Structuring:** Multiple ATM deposits just under the reporting limit

## Risk Assessment
- **High Risk:** Repeated pattern of just-below-threshold transactions with no legitimate business explanation
- **Medium Risk:** Occasional below-threshold transactions with partial business justification
- **Low Risk:** Isolated transaction that happens to fall below threshold with clear legitimate purpose

## Regulatory References
- Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) — 31 U.S.C. § 5324
- FinCEN Advisory on Structuring (FIN-2014-A007)
